1. Publish only the public directory: index.html, styles.css, app.js, vision.js, live.js, logo.svg, favicon.svg, jonathan-kim.jpg, and fonts/. The site needs no build step or package install.
2. Serve index.html for the root path. Serve app.js, vision.js, and live.js as text/javascript, styles.css as text/css, fonts/*.woff2 as font/woff2, logo.svg and favicon.svg as image/svg+xml, and jonathan-kim.jpg as image/jpeg.
3. Fonts are self-hosted Newsreader and Hanken Grotesk variable fonts from @fontsource-variable (SIL Open Font License). Do not load fonts from Google.
4. The page has five snap-scrolling stages and a right-side section index: Live demo, The vision, The voice, Where it stands, About us.
5. The live demo plays a scam chat (each new message gets typing dots, then stays up long enough to read), a ScamCatch warning, and a "Tap here to talk" voice screen, then replays after 16 seconds without interaction. Tapping opens the call screen. The "Pick a scam to watch" buttons under the progress bar switch between eight scenarios (romance: Simon offers to match $40,000 in a Monero fund, bank login favor for $100,000, false charge (a text about a charge you didn't make, then a fake fraud department that has you empty your account into a "protected" one and coaches you to lie to your bank about why), tech support, IRS, jury duty, lottery/Publishers Clearing House, fake Brad Pitt asking for $20,000, with stand-in silhouette photos rather than real photos of him). The scenario text lives in SCENARIOS in public/app.js; ?scam=<id> opens on one (for example scamcatch.io/?scam=irs). The live voice gets whichever chat is on screen.
6. live.js defines window.ScamCatchLive.start(context, { onStatus, onCaption }), which returns a session with end(). Without live.js, the call screen says live voice isn't connected.
7. The live voice context is the chat shown in the demo. ScamCatchDemo.context() returns it.
8. The demo chats use three looks, set per scam by "app" in SCENARIOS (public/app.js): romance is a dark WhatsApp-style chat, bank favor and celebrity are a dark Messenger-style chat, and the rest are iMessage-style. They are styled after those apps only. Do not use Apple, iMessage, WhatsApp, Facebook, Messenger, or Meta logos. The romance contact is named Simon. Unknown senders use fictional 555-01xx numbers. Real company and celebrity names appear only as the scammer's claim, never with their logos or photos.
9. The vision stage (id "vision") is the map, under the headline "A first line of defense on every phone in America." It is called The vision, not How it works, because it includes something the app does not do yet (trace & investigate to help get back lost funds). The map is the lower 48 from us-atlas 3 states-10m (U.S. Census Bureau, ISC license), projected with d3-geo geoAlbers into a 960x610 viewBox and inlined in index.html. METROS in vision.js use that projection. vision.js draws about 34 people holding smartphones on their left. One scam at a time flies in from an ocean edge to one of the eight STORIES people and turns that phone red, and every scam gets the card; there are no scams without it. The next scam starts once the card has closed. The card follows each scam in four steps, and each card section opens as its step starts: (1) the scam: city, scam type, and the flagged message from SCENARIOS in app.js (exposed as ScamCatchDemo.scenarios); (2) the catch: the ScamCatch logo pops up over the phone and pulls the scam into it, and the card shows the first sentence of the alert beside the logo. There is no decoy anywhere on the page; (3) the reports: traces fly one at a time to three icons near the geographic center of the lower 48 (HUBS in vision.js: Bank, Authorities, Trusted contacts), and each card row (Your bank, The authorities, Your trusted contacts) shows Sent as its trace lands, on that same frame. The card says reports go out with the person's OK; keep that wording; (4) trace & investigate to help get back lost funds, in violet (--trace in styles.css): a trail hops from the phone back along the scam's route through three crypto wallets to a magnifying glass near the edge of the map, labeled "Investigating" once the trail reaches it, and the card's "Trace & investigate" chain lights up hop by hop and says "Traced to help get the money back" and "Investigating". Never name or promise an exchange. Beside the headline, a four-step timeline, each step an icon with its number as a badge (a red text bubble; the ScamCatch logo; the card's bank, authorities and trusted contacts icons, copied from the card by vision.js; a violet trail of wallets) (a scammer messages you; ScamCatch intervenes and opens a voice chat; with your OK, it alerts your bank, the authorities, and your trusted contacts; trace & investigate to help get back lost funds) lights each step as the card reaches it, and each icon in step 3 hops and lights up as its report lands. On desktop the headline row reaches into the room kept for the section index, as the map does; on small screens the timeline is two rows of two. The Authorities icon is an original eagle spread across a shield with an olive branch on each side, not any agency's seal; the card's SVG paths are the one shape, and vision.js draws them on the map too. No person is placed in the zone around the icons (HUB_ZONE). Each report trace ends at the edge of its icon. The card's spot is picked before the scam launches, and the scam's route is chosen to stay clear of the card and the icons, so the crypto trail stays in view. The card shrinks to fit short maps. Icon labels hide when they would overlap. The cities, people, and attempts are illustrative, not live data. It animates only while on screen and shows a still frame with all four steps done under reduced motion.
10. The About us stage (id "about") is the last stage: Jonathan's photo (jonathan-kim.jpg, cropped to 4:5, metadata stripped) beside "Hi, I'm Jonathan.", his background (software engineer from Diamond Bar, California; left his embedded systems grad program at UC Irvine to build ScamCatch), what he has learned from scam survivors, what ScamCatch is for, Mirae Opus LLC (Mirae means "future" in Korean), and the mission: take the money lost to scams down to zero, until scams are a thing of the past, like smallpox and polio. The site footer sits at the bottom of this stage. On small screens the photo sits above the text.
11. The website has no contact form, email address, analytics, or advertising scripts.
12. Start Website.cmd runs server.py (Python 3.11+, standard library only). It serves public/ at http://localhost:5180 with Cache-Control: no-store and adds POST /api/voice/session.
13. The endpoint takes {"sdp", "context"} and returns {"sdp", "greeting", "noBackendCommentary"}, or {"error"} with a short message. context is ScamCatchDemo.context().
14. Before voice works, fill in voiceKeySecret.secretId and voiceKeySecret.region in infra/deployment.json. jsonField is optional. The server window says what is missing.
15. Voice prompts, key handling, and cost limits are in docs/voice.md under Website voice rules.
16. On scamcatch.io, /api/* goes through CloudFront to the scamcatch-voice Lambda (infra/voice/handler.py), which reads the same secret and mirrors server.py. It allows 50 calls per UTC day and one new call every 10 seconds, counted in the scamcatch-voice-usage DynamoDB table. Deploy Voice.cmd in the repository root builds and deploys it with infra/deploy_voice.py, then checks the endpoint without starting a call. Results go to infra/voice-deploy-log.txt. Run it again after editing AIInstructions.kt or handler.py.
